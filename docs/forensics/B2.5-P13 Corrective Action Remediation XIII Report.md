## 2. Pre-remediation falsifier

On the exact starting main, the production builder created a schema-valid
available-confidence envelope. Only `runtime_policy_version` was then forged.
The neutral provenance validator refused it, but the direct production signer
minted an Ed25519 signature and signature hash; public-only JWKS verification
accepted the result:

```text
builder_status=success
forged_schema_validation=valid
provenance_validator=REFUSED:policy_bundle_tuple_mismatch:runtime_policy_version
direct_mismatched_payload_signature=SUCCEEDED
mismatched_payload_public_verification=verified
signature_present=True
signature_hash_present=True
```

The signer validated signature metadata, generated canonical hashes, and then
called `private_key.sign()`. It never resolved the consequence-bearing policy
tuple. Builder validation was therefore a convention, not an authority
boundary, and direct callers could authenticate a semantic contradiction. The
required P13 context was green-capable because no upstream physical test called
the signer with forged provenance.

## 3. Remediation: one inescapable signing boundary

`prepare_payload_for_signing()` now invokes the neutral
`validate_envelope_policy_authority()` before computing new hashes or reaching
the private-key signing call. For available confidence it requires and resolves:

- policy bundle content identity;
- inference, runtime, sampling, diagnostic, confidence-policy, and confidence-semantics versions;
- authorized and observed chain correspondence;
- authorized and observed posterior-draw correspondence;
- current-issuance status of the resolved bundle.

Any contradiction raises a typed `policy_authority_refused` error. The tested
counting private key records zero signing calls and the caller’s envelope remains
byte-for-byte unchanged. Deterministic-only envelopes may honestly carry null
inference provenance; degraded envelopes may omit it, but any provenance they do
name must still resolve internally.

An AST-based open-world inventory finds every `.sign(...)` call under
`backend/app`. Exactly three governed domains exist: TrustEnvelope,
TrustExportArtifact, and TrustQueryContinuation. Only
`backend/app/trust/signing.py` is a TrustEnvelope signer, and its mandatory
boundary is mechanically ordered before the crypto call. Adding a sibling route
or a second TrustEnvelope signer makes the C13 gate red.
